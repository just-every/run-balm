# Balm contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Balm is the practice management system and EHR a US outpatient physical, occupational or speech therapy practice buys instead of WebPT, SimplePractice, Jane or Cliniko, with the rehab-specific billing and AI depth buyers now look for in SPRY, Prompt Health and splose. It runs the whole clinic: scheduling, intake and the patient portal, discipline-specific documentation, reminders and two-way messaging, and cash-pay and insurance billing through to money in the bank. Prices, fees and balances are in USD; dates, phone numbers, time zones, tax IDs and payer rules follow US practice.

This is a target contract, not a release verdict. It describes the product Balm is being built towards, and the [Status](#status) section marks how close the current code is. Every named clause states the behavior a buyer gets and the acceptance evidence that proves it through a public door. Difficulty (clearinghouse enrollment, payer rules, HIPAA business associate agreements, native apps, card payments) is engineering work recorded as a clause, never a reason to narrow the product. The released guarantees, which the earlier contract carried under `BALM-001`, its paragraphs, `BALM-002`, `BALM-003` and `BALM-004`, are kept below with their behavior unchanged; this documentation round changes no homepage, interface or runtime behavior.

## Product target

| | |

| Category | Practice management and EHR for therapy / allied-health clinics |

WebPT, SimplePractice, Jane and Cliniko are the incumbents; SPRY, Prompt Health and splose are the up-and-comers Balm must also beat. A practice switches when every daily job has a Balm equivalent, its records arrive with balances that reconcile, and its payers, card processor, books and phones plug in:

- **Schedule.** Practitioner, room and equipment calendars, recurring visits that follow the plan of care, waitlists that fill cancellations by text, patient self-booking and rescheduling, and attendance history (`SCHED-*`).
- **Welcome the patient.** Intake packets, signed consents and insurance cards collected before the first visit, and a portal for appointments, home exercises, messages, statements and payments (`INTAKE-*`, `PORTAL-*`).
- **Document.** PT, OT and SLP evaluations, daily notes, progress reports, re-evaluations and discharge summaries; plans of care certified by the referring physician by link or returned fax, with unsigned and late plans tracked; objective measures and outcome scores; sign, lock, amend and co-sign; an ambient scribe that drafts for the clinician to sign (`BALM-004`, `CHART-*`).
- **Coordinate care.** Referrals and referring providers, faxes in and out, and direct-access limits (`REF-*`).
- **Stay in touch.** Delivered email and two-way SMS reminders, confirmations, follow-ups and recalls, recorded on the patient's file (`COMM-*`, `REMIND-001`), and video visits from the same calendar (`TELE-001`).
- **Get paid by patients.** Cards on file through Stripe, copays at check-in, text-to-pay links, statements, superbills, Good Faith Estimates, packages and payment plans (`BILL-001`, `BALM-003`, `PAY-*`).
- **Get paid by payers.** Payers and fee schedules, eligibility, authorizations, clean claims through Claim.MD or Availity, ERA posting, denials and A/R follow-up, and Medicare's 8-minute rule, KX threshold, ABN and assistant modifiers (`INS-*`).
- **Run the business.** Daily close, operational, financial and outcome reports, and QuickBooks Online or Xero handoff (`REPORT-*`, `ACCT-001`).
- **Stay safe.** Live roles, two-step sign-in, an append-only access audit, HIPAA deployment readiness, retention, disclosure accounting, backups, erasure and complete export (`BALM-001`, `SEC-*`, `AUDIT-001`, `HIPAA-*`, `ERASE-001`, `PORT-001`).
- **Move in.** Real migrations from rehabilitation EHRs, clinic management systems, therapy practice systems and allied-health practice systems with reconciled patients, appointments, documents, balances and open authorizations (`MOVE-*`).
- **Anywhere.** A tablet-first clinician web app and native iOS and Android apps for clinicians and patients (`MOBILE-*`), a public API, webhooks and FHIR export (`INTEG-*`).
- **Push further.** A case agent that follows each referral from authorization to paid claim, plus agents for scheduling continuity, documentation defensibility, denials, month-end reconciliation, outcomes, the front-desk inbox and migration, each under human control (`AGENT-*`).

### How to read this contract

- **Published clauses** are the behavior the released contract already promised: `BALM-001`, `BALM-002`, `BALM-003` and `BALM-004` keep their IDs, and the guarantees that contract stated in its paragraphs and in `ACCEPTANCE.md` are named here (`SETUP-001`, `SCHED-001`, `INTAKE-001`, `CHART-001`, `BILL-001`, `PAY-001`, `REMIND-001`, `AUDIT-001`, `ERASE-001`, `PORT-001`, `MOVE-001`). Public-door integration coverage is in `tests/balm.test.mjs`, `tests/schedule.test.mjs`, `tests/payments.test.mjs` (Stripe and Twilio faked only at their HTTP edge) and `tests/charting.test.mjs` (the scribe's model faked only at its HTTP edge); CLI archive coverage is in `tests/archive.test.mjs`. Stripe is the only payment provider today, and the scribe calls only the AI provider whose key the owner entered.
- **Target clauses** are binding product requirements not yet promised by a release. A build round that starts one registers it in `seed.json → capabilities` in the same change. IDs never change.
- **Push-further clauses** (`AGENT-*`) are agent work beyond today's up-and-comers. Every agent acts through `BALM-001`'s prepare-and-approve fence: it reads only what its key's scope allows, prepares, and a named person approves; clinicians confirm every clinical assertion and people decide every financial exception.
- **Status** is `done`, `partial` or `missing`, judged in the [Status](#status) section from the code and tests at the commit it names. `done` means the behavior ships and a cited test exercises it; `partial` means some of it ships; `missing` means there is no implementation. Status is a build-planning judgment, not acceptance; [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md) lists each clause's acceptance evidence.

## Existing guarantees

- **BALM-001 — Live roles and server-side authority.** Every `/app` page and `/api/balm` handler resolves the live actor from a native session (or, at `/api/balm/agent/*`, an owner-bound agent key) and enforces its role in the handler; every write re-checks that authority inside the same D1 batch. Roles: the owner; practitioners, who see only clients they are booked with; reception, who see every file but never intake answers or treatment notes; and agent keys, which only prepare work for a person. Balances, copays and card details are visible to the owner and reception by default. The owner can grant balance visibility to a named active practitioner and revoke it; each grant and revocation is audited and takes effect on the next request. The grant adds only balances for patients already within that practitioner’s scope, not copay or card details or payment powers. A person approves every preparation, and the approval keeps the SHA-256 of the exact approved content. Deactivating staff or revoking a key takes effect on the next request. **Acceptance evidence:** Call every page and handler signed out, as each role, with a deactivated staff member and with a revoked or foreign agent key; each is refused or scoped by role, and an agent-prepared item is approved only by a signed-in person whose approval records the digest.

- **BALM-002 — Calendar, recurring series and waitlist offers.** The schedule is a calendar by day or week and by practitioner or room in the clinic time zone. Staff book, move (the edit form or a drag) and cancel visits; every booking, move, recurring series and waitlist acceptance re-checks in the same D1 batch that no other live visit of that practitioner or that room overlaps, and staff placements stay inside the practitioner's hours. A recurring series is checked date by date before anything is saved: a conflicting date is shown and saves only once a person moved or skipped it, the whole series books in one batch, and skipped dates are recorded. One visit or the rest of a series can be moved or canceled; moving the rest changes none if any would conflict. The waitlist holds clients per practitioner (or any) and service in a date window. A canceled visit at least two hours ahead is offered by email to every fitting entry through a private `/offer/<token>` link (only its hash is stored); the first acceptance books the visit and closes the offer in one batch, later ones are refused, and the others are emailed that it is taken. Offers close one hour before the visit. Text-message waitlist offers remain unimplemented; the SMS adapter currently supports reminders and pay links (`COMM-002`). The practitioner's concurrent-patient capacity (`SCHED-010`) defaults to one, which is this overlap rule. **Acceptance evidence:** Book a recurring series whose date overlaps an existing visit, see that date shown before saving, move or skip it and book the series in one step; move one visit, then move and cancel the rest of the series; cancel a visit at least two hours ahead, see it offered by email to every fitting waitlist entry, accept as one, see the visit booked, a second acceptance refused and the others emailed that it is taken.

- **BALM-003 — Patient payments through the clinic's own Stripe account.** Patients pay through the clinic's own Stripe account; card numbers never reach Balm. The owner connects a Stripe secret key in Clinic (checked with Stripe, sealed with the session secret, never shown again, never exported); on a public https install Balm creates the webhook. A patient saves one card on file from their private `/pay/<token>` link (30 days, only its hash stored, never exported) after accepting the clinic's authorization, whose exact text and time are kept with the card (Stripe Checkout setup mode, a SetupIntent for off-session use). The desk checks a patient in on the day of the visit and, in the same action, charges the patient's copay to the card off session, once per visit; a refusal is recorded and the visit stays checked in. The desk charges an approved invoice's open amount to the card, or sends the patient a pay link by email (by text through the SMS adapter once the clinic's US A2P registration and Twilio settings exist); the patient pays any amount up to their balance in Stripe Checkout. Every outcome is read back from Stripe (on return, by signed webhook) and a payment posts once: one receipt number, and one `bm_payments` row per invoice with the reference `stripe:<intent>:<invoice>`, never above the invoice; a copay waits on its visit and the rest of any payment is credit, both applied when the next invoice is approved. The patient's link shows a statement across visits (charges, payments, running balance) and gives the statement, receipts and per-visit superbills as PDFs; a superbill carries the clinic's tax ID, the practitioner's NPI, the ICD-10 diagnoses and CPT codes with modifiers and units (the signed note's billed units when present) and is refused until all are present. Every check-in, saved or removed card, charge, link, opening and download is audited for the patient. On RER Managed hosting, which has no egress relay yet, connecting Stripe is refused and every payment surface says in one line that online payment is not available yet. `PAY-002` to `PAY-005` carry the rest of each job. **Acceptance evidence:** On a self-hosted install with Stripe faked at its HTTP edge, save a card from the patient's link with the authorization, check the patient in and charge the copay in one action with a receipt, send a pay link by email and pay the balance, and see each payment posted to its invoice once, on the statement and on the superbill; replay the Stripe webhook and see nothing posted twice; on RER Managed see the one line.

- **BALM-004 — Templates, signed notes, plan-of-care link and scribe drafts.** Treatment notes start from the template set on the visit's service: PT, OT and speech-language initial evaluation, daily note, progress report and discharge summary, each with subjective, objective measures, assessment, goals and plan sections. A note is a draft, saved as revisions, until its author signs the exact latest revision with their typed name and credentials; signing records the time and the SHA-256 of what was signed, is refused while a required section is empty, and locks the note, which the database enforces; later changes are addenda signed when written. Each note records CPT codes with minutes; billed units follow the CMS 8-minute rule (timed codes on their total minutes, whole units first and the rest to the most minutes left over; untimed codes one unit), and an invoice carries the units of its visit's signed notes. The schedule shows each visit's note as signed, not signed or missing and lists attended visits without a signed note. A plan of care (diagnoses, goals, frequency, duration, interventions) is signed by its therapist and sent to the referring physician through a private `/plan/<token>` link (only its hash is stored, expiring in 30 days); the physician signs once without an account, the plan's content never changes once sent, and the case shows signed or not with the date. The scribe drafts a note in the visit's template from the practitioner's dictation or bullet points with the install's own AI key, which the owner enters on the Clinic page and Balm keeps sealed; it sends no patient name or birth date, says "Add a key to use the scribe." without a key, never signs, and on RER Managed hosting says it is not available yet. `CHART-002` to `CHART-006` and `CHART-012` carry the rest of each job. **Acceptance evidence:** Write a PT daily note from the visit's template, try to sign with a required section empty and be refused, sign it and see it locked with an addendum allowed; record 38 minutes across two timed codes and see 3 billed units on the note and its invoice; send a plan of care by link, sign it as the physician and see it signed with the date on the case and the schedule; draft a note with the scribe from dictation with a faked model and see an unsigned draft, and without a key see the one line.

- **SETUP-001 — Clinic, services, practitioners and hours.** The owner sets the clinic name, address, tax ID and time zone, services with length and price, practitioners with their discipline and weekly hours, and invites practitioners and reception by email; a first-run checklist shows what is left. The time zone starts from the owner's browser time zone and can change later: booked visits keep their exact moment, weekly hours keep their clock times, and the owner confirms the change when visits are booked. **Acceptance evidence:** From a fresh install, complete the checklist through the app, invite a practitioner and a receptionist, and see the public booking page offer only the practitioner's hours.

- **SCHED-001 — Online booking from real open times.** New patients book at `/book` from a practitioner's real open times in 15-minute steps, at least 2 hours and at most 60 days ahead, get the intake link by email, and a taken or invalid slot is refused in the same D1 batch that inserts the appointment, even when two people race for it. **Acceptance evidence:** Book online for a new patient, see the slot disappear and the intake email sent, book a taken or off-grid slot and be refused, and race two bookings for one slot and see exactly one succeed.

- **INTAKE-001 — One-use intake bound to its form version.** A booking emails a private intake link through the clinic's mail only when the visit is in the future and the patient has neither a submitted intake for the clinic's current published form version nor an open, unexpired intake link. For request reuse, an open link belongs to an unfinished booked visit whose scheduled end has not passed, with a stored token hash and no submitted response for that visit. The link remains usable after the scheduled start (including in the waiting room) and refuses at the scheduled end, on completion (attended), or on cancellation, whichever happens first. An unanswered link shows the current published form; publication during completion refuses the stale submission and asks the patient to reload. Reuse that link, never duplicate it. A booking entered for a past date never emails anything. After the clinic publishes a new form version, the next booking for a patient whose intake answered an older version emails a fresh link for the current one. Each link accepts answers once, binds them to the exact form version answered, never echoes clinical answers back, and lets only the treating practitioner and the owner read them. **Acceptance evidence:** Book online, open the emailed link, submit the intake, replay the submission and be refused; reopen the link and see no clinical answers echoed back; read the answers as the treating practitioner and owner and be refused as reception and as another practitioner. A returning patient with a submitted intake for the current form version books and receives no new intake link. A patient with an open, unexpired link books again and the same link stands, without a duplicate. Enter a past-dated booking and see no email sent. After the clinic publishes a new form version, book a patient whose submitted intake answered the older version and see a fresh link emailed for the current version; the earlier answers stay bound to the exact version answered.

- **CHART-001 — Private revisioned treatment notes.** Treatment notes are readable only by their author and the owner (and, under `CHART-013`, the care team the practice grants), and amendments add revisions that keep every earlier text. **Acceptance evidence:** Write and amend a note as a practitioner; read every revision as the author and the owner, and be refused as reception and another practitioner.

- **REMIND-001 — Reminder content a person approves.** Reminder text is prepared from the appointment (by staff or an agent key), a person approves the exact text, and the approval keeps its digest; the approved text can be exported unchanged. Delivery is `COMM-001` and `COMM-002`. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare a reminder with an agent key, approve it as a person, and export the exact approved text with the approval digest recorded.

- **BILL-001 — Approved, numbered invoices with fixed bytes.** An invoice is prepared from an attended visit, a person approves it, and only then is it numbered and its downloadable bytes fixed; approval is refused if any frozen fact changed; voiding keeps the number. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare, change the visit, see approval refused, re-prepare and approve; download the invoice twice and get identical bytes.

- **PAY-001 — Payments recorded once per reference.** Payments received outside Balm are recorded with a unique external reference, never above the invoice, and leave the outstanding balance. **Acceptance evidence:** Record a check payment, record the same reference again and be refused, and try to exceed the invoice and be refused.

- **AUDIT-001 — Append-only access audit.** `bm_audit` is append-only. Opening a patient file, reading intake answers or notes, every change, every invoice or reminder download and every agent read of patient names add rows for that patient; audit rows carry no patient personal details; only the owner reads them. **Acceptance evidence:** Open a file, read intake and notes, download an invoice and read names with an agent key; the file's audit lists each, and an attempt to update or delete an audit row is refused.

- **ERASE-001 — Erasure fenced against old backups.** The owner erases a patient or staff member who has no health records; the erasure is journaled so a restore or import cannot bring them back, and a patient with health records is retained under `HIPAA-002`. **Acceptance evidence:** Erase an unused patient and staff member, restore an older archive and re-import the patient, and see both refused; try to erase a treated patient and be refused.

- **PORT-001 — Whole-clinic export and restore.** `rer export` writes the whole clinic as an archive; `rer import` restores it into an empty destination and refuses damaged archives and populated stores. **Acceptance evidence:** Export, damage a copy and see it refused, import into a populated store and see it refused, import into an empty one and see the clinic intact.

- **MOVE-001 — Reviewed patient import with undo.** The owner imports patients from an allied-health practice system patient CSV: preview every row with diagnostics and conflicts, apply exactly the reviewed preview, read the journal with the original file, and undo without losing patients who have since been booked or edited. **Acceptance evidence:** Preview, apply and undo a synthetic allied-health practice system file in which one patient was booked after import; the booked patient is kept and the journal says why.

## Target clauses

### Clinic setup, roles and security

- **ROLE-002 — Billing, assistant, student and supervisor roles.** Beyond owner, practitioner and reception, the practice assigns biller (claims, payments and A/R, no note text beyond what a claim needs), PTA/COTA/SLPA assistants (document under a supervising therapist), students and clinical fellows (draft only, co-signature required), and location managers (one location's schedule and reports). The biller sees `REPORT-002`'s financial reports, which the owner alone sees until then. Each permission is enforced server-side as `BALM-001` does today. **ct-approved baseline (22 October 2026):** the owner assigns the job and named active licensed supervisor; billers have billing-only access, students and fellows save and submit their own drafts but never sign independently; assistants do the same unless an explicit CHART-008 state/payer daily-signing rule permits their own final daily signature, and location managers have schedule and visit-count access to their one location (rooms must be assigned before use). The exact current matrix is `docs/ACCESS-MATRIX.md`; every new command defaults to refusal for these narrower jobs. Assistants never author an evaluation, re-evaluation, progress report, discharge summary or plan of care, or charge an evaluation code; students and clinical fellows draft them for co-signature. A location manager books, reschedules, cancels and checks in visits in their location's rooms only; each write rechecks the assigned location in its transaction. The manager books existing patients already scheduled at that location; the owner books a new patient’s first visit there. Check-in never charges a card. Billers prepare the signed notes for selected visits against a recorded payer policy and a records-request or appeal reference; the owner reviews and approves the exact original PDF before biller download, which expires after seven days or immediate owner revocation. No outbound send is performed. **Acceptance evidence:** Sign in as each new role and attempt every read and write in the access matrix; allowed actions succeed, others are refused with no write, and each refusal is audited.

- **SEC-001 — Two-step sign-in and session control.** Staff sign in with an email link plus a second step (passkey or authenticator code), required for every role that can read PHI; an owner can require it clinic-wide, sees active sessions and devices, signs any of them out, and sets an idle timeout (default 15 minutes on shared workstations). Balm requires an independent authenticator code for the owner, administrators and every active clinic staff job before opening patient information, including location schedules. The first verified setup is bound to that signed-in identity and device; the encrypted seed never travels in archives. Enrollment and regeneration each show ten one-use recovery codes once; there is no email-only reset or owner bypass. A used authenticator time step or recovery code cannot be replayed. Native sessions expire on their next request after the owner-set idle period (1–120 minutes, default 15), and owner device revocation takes effect on the next request. ct-approved enrollment and recovery (22 October 2026): all PHI-reading jobs require this independent second step. Recovery codes are stored only as salted hashes; generating a new set with a fresh authenticator code invalidates every previous code. Each use is audited and shown on the private Staff security page at the next sign-in, with no email or SMS notice. Factors, recovery credentials and session proofs are excluded from archives, and restored users enroll again. Losing the authenticator and every recovery code leaves the user without PHI access until re-enrolled; no reset or owner bypass is implemented. Optional OIDC or SAML sign-in through the practice's identity provider (Auth, Google Workspace, Okta) maps to Balm roles without widening them. **Acceptance evidence:** Enforce two-step sign-in, sign in with and without the second step, let a session idle past the timeout, and sign out a device remotely; the next request from each refused session is denied.

- **SEC-002 — Public doors that refuse abuse.** `/book`, `/intake/<token>`, portal sign-in and payment links check request origin, rate-limit by address and by patient email, cap pending online bookings per email, never echo clinical answers, and treat every link token as a one-use or expiring bearer credential stored only as a hash. Exact semantics (ct, 20 October 2026): aligned 15-minute windows keep secret-salted hashed counters, separate by door and address/patient. Owner-visible defaults are booking 30 requests/address and 10/normalized submitted email; intake and payment 120/address and 60/server-resolved patient email, including page, action and document requests; portal sign-in 20/address and 5/email, and confirmation 30/address and 5/server-resolved email. A patient without email uses the same patient quota keyed by a secret-salted opaque client ID; invalid or unresolved tokens take only the address quota. Counters are atomic and contain no raw address, email or client ID. CF-Connecting-IP is trusted only with Cloudflare request metadata and outside local mode; otherwise requests share one hashed installation bucket and forwarded headers cannot choose another. Origin rules remain unchanged; signed provider webhooks are excluded. A denied request causes no domain or provider effect: portal sign-in keeps its generic notice, and other doors return 429 without patient details or clinical answers. The three-future-online-bookings-per-email cap is checked in the booking transaction, including concurrent requests. Future public capabilities under `SCHED-007`, `TELE-001`, `PAY-002`/`PAY-003`, `PORTAL-001` and `MOVE-*` inherit these obligations before shipping. **Acceptance evidence:** Post to each public door cross-origin, replay a used intake link, exceed the booking cap and the rate limit, and inspect storage; each is refused and no raw token is stored.

- **US-001 — US clinic defaults.** A new clinic starts in USD with en-US dates, a US time zone chosen at setup, +1 phone numbers, US states and ZIP codes, an EIN or SSN tax ID with the practice NPI, and US imports reading MM/DD/YYYY dates. **Acceptance evidence:** Set up a clinic in America/Chicago, book, invoice and import a patient file with US dates; every screen, file and message shows USD, en-US dates, +1 numbers and the practice's EIN and NPI.

- **OWN-001 — Owned, open-source and free.** Balm is MIT licensed and charges nothing. The practice runs it on its own computer (`rer install`, `rer setup`, `rer dev`) or its own Cloudflare account, its database is its own, and no license check, telemetry or kill switch exists. Real patient data runs only on a deployment `HIPAA-005` names, with its hosting BAA (or practice-owned hardware) recorded; any other install stays in demo mode with synthetic patients. **Acceptance evidence:** Install from a clean checkout with the documented commands, run the clinic loop, and observe no outbound request other than the declared integrations the owner enabled. With no hosting record, attempt real-patient creation through the app, `/book`, intake, import and migration and see each refused; complete and remove the hosting record and verify the audited real-patient switch and renewed refusal required by `HIPAA-005`. Acceptance depends on both no undeclared outbound requests and `HIPAA-005` real-patient gating; a no-outbound test alone cannot complete `OWN-001`.

### Scheduling

- **SCHED-002 — Practitioner, room and equipment calendar.** Staff work from a day and week calendar across practitioners, treatment rooms, gyms and equipment (for example a pool or an AlterG), drag to move a visit, see intake, authorization and balance flags on each visit, and print or export the day sheet. A visit can require a room or equipment and is refused when it is taken. Visits a practitioner carries at the same time under their capacity (`SCHED-010`) show side by side in that practitioner's day and week column, each with its own patient, status and flags. Exact semantics: a visit names at most one room and at most one equipment item, and each holds one booked or attended visit at any instant; staff booking and single moves choose them, series moves and patient moves (portal and reminder links) keep them, and patient doors neither offer nor take a time when the visit's room or equipment is held. Drag moves follow the edit form's authority: the owner and reception move any booked visit to any practitioner, room or equipment; a practitioner moves only booked visits in their own diary and only within it. The intake flag reads received (for this visit), on file (an earlier answer) or due; the authorization flag is the authorization covering the visit's clinic-local date, its remaining visits being authorized minus attended minus booked as in `INS-003`; the balance flag is what the patient owes on approved invoices with no claim pending, shown under `BALM-001` to the owner, reception and practitioners with the owner’s balance grant. The day sheet is one clinic-local day of the calendar the actor may see, with the same practitioner, room or equipment filter, as a printable page and a CSV; agent keys get neither. Each future visit-placing door (`SCHED-007` locations, `TELE-001` video visits, `MOVE-*` imported visits) inherits the room and equipment refusal before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** Book two practitioners into one room at the same time and see the second refused; book the AlterG over a visit that already holds it and see that refused; move a visit by drag and see its history; read the week view for three practitioners with each visit's flags; book two patients at 9:00 for a PT whose capacity is 2 and see both side by side in the PT's column; print and export the day sheet and see the same visits and flags.

- **SCHED-003 — Recurring visit series.** A practitioner books a series (for example twice weekly for 6 weeks) from the plan of care's frequency and duration; Balm names every conflict before booking, books the rest, and lets staff edit or cancel one visit, this and following, or the whole series. **Acceptance evidence:** Book a 12-visit series that collides with a holiday and an existing visit; see the conflicts named, resolve them, and cancel "this and following" from visit 8 with earlier visits untouched. The plan-derived pattern starts on the plan’s start-of-care date, lasts its duration in whole weeks and requires exactly its frequency of selected weekdays; reviewed exceptions may move a date or skip it explicitly. Clinic holidays are named full local dates; a time-zone change recalculates their boundaries in the new clinic zone. Existing visits are retained for staff to resolve. One, this-and-following and whole-series actions operate on booked visits, retaining completed visits and the recorded skipped dates.

- **SCHED-004 — Reschedule, cancel and no-show policy.** Staff and patients reschedule a visit without losing its history, intake or authorization link; cancellations record who and why; the clinic sets a late-cancel window and a late-cancel or no-show fee that is charged to the card on file only after a person confirms. The owner configures notice in elapsed hours and whole-cent USD fees (zero disables a fee). A cancellation is late only when time until its start is strictly less than the notice window; equality is outside it. Private visit-link patients choose real quarter-hour slots with the same practitioner and service, inside the notice, availability and authorization rules; successful moves revoke the old bearer and email a fresh link, while the visit ID, intake and authorization reservation remain. Cancellation requires a reason for single and series commands and records the acting person; no-show attendance records its actor too. Owner or reception separately records explicit patient authorization for the particular fee and confirms the frozen proposal before charging its saved card, once under replay or racing confirmation. Old card-saving consent is never broadened. Without authorization nothing is charged and cancellation still succeeds. **Acceptance evidence:** Reschedule a visit and see one appointment with its history; cancel inside the late window and see the fee proposed, approved and charged once; mark a no-show and see it in attendance reports.

- **SCHED-005 — Waitlist that fills openings.** Patients join a waitlist with practitioner, days, times and earliest date; when a visit is canceled, Balm offers the opening by text and email to matching patients in order, holds it for a set time, books the first patient who accepts and tells the others it is gone. **Acceptance evidence:** Put three patients on the waitlist, cancel a matching visit, accept from the second patient's text reply after the first lets the hold lapse, and see one booking and a "slot taken" message to the third.

- **SCHED-006 — Patient self-service scheduling.** Existing patients book, reschedule and cancel follow-up visits from the portal, a reminder reply or `/book` (where an exact name, birth date and email match continues their file) within clinic rules: which services they may self-book, notice, and whether an active plan of care or authorization is required. Self-booking offers only times when the practitioner has no live visit, unless the practice allows online double-booking for that service under `SCHED-010`. The booking page embeds on the clinic's website. The portal books, moves and cancels the session patient’s own visits and offers the same published active practitioners and services as `/book`; a reminder link moves or cancels only its own visit. The owner configures each service’s self-booking flag, minimum notice (elapsed minutes, at least 120), and optional certified-plan and authorization requirements. A required plan must belong to that patient and practitioner and cover the visit’s clinic-local date, including its start and end dates; an authorization must cover that date and practitioner with an unreserved visit, and an existing expired or exhausted authorization is refused even when authorizations are optional. Moves release their own prior reservation. Moves also obey `SCHED-004`’s elapsed-hour notice at both the old and new time; cancellations obey it at the original time, and patients cannot override fees. Cancellation remains available outside notice if new self-booking is disabled. Authority, identity, clinic rules, clinical eligibility and capacity are rechecked in the same write batch. Ambiguous exact returning identities ask the patient to contact the clinic; concurrent first bookings cannot create duplicate matching patient files. The owner explicitly allowlists HTTPS website origins (HTTP loopback only in local development); only `/book` may be framed, the host cannot read its form, and final submission leaves the frame before a private destination. `/book` still requires the exact Balm Origin, including ancestor-tainted framed requests; every other Origin rule stays unchanged. `SCHED-010` inherits these patient-door capacity and Medicare obligations before online double-booking ships. **Acceptance evidence:** As a returning patient, book at `/book` and land in the same file; as a portal patient, book a follow-up, reschedule it inside the rules, and be refused outside notice or past the authorized visit count; book through the page embedded on a test website.

- **SCHED-007 — Service types, locations and availability rules.** Services carry their own length, price, CPT defaults and bookable practitioners (an evaluation at 60 minutes, a follow-up at 45); a practice runs several locations with their own hours, rooms and time zone; holidays, time off and one-day overrides change availability; buffers separate visits. Before locations ship, `REPORT-001` reports filter by location. Current semantics: the owner sets each service’s practitioner and location lists (empty lists retain unrestricted legacy services), up to six suggested CPT codes and 0–120-minute before/after buffers. A selected room identifies its location; explicitly configured locations intersect their hours with the practitioner’s weekly clock times in that location’s zone. Older report-only locations retain clinic hours until configured. Public and portal booking show location-local times; the staff calendar, staff entry, series patterns and reports retain clinic-local dates and times. Named date rules close or replace a location’s, practitioner’s or combined scope’s hours; every applicable scope must permit the visit and clinic holidays still win. Adjacent visits reserve the sum of their services’ buffers, even across locations; buffers disallow overlapping visits when either service reserves them. Rule changes retain existing visits; every new booking or move rechecks current rules and a same-batch rule revision and buffer guard. CPT suggestions only prefill a clinician-reviewed draft and record no treatment. Existing original-record imports retain their source times; `MOVE-002` through `MOVE-005` inherit service/location mapping and conflict review before expanding their scheduling doors. **Acceptance evidence:** Create two locations, an evaluation and a follow-up service and a day off; online booking offers each service only where and when its rules allow.

- **SCHED-008 — Check-in, attendance and change history.** Each visit moves through booked, checked in, attended, no-show or canceled, and every change is recorded with who and when. Marking a visit attended atomically opens a blank draft note for the treating practitioner (retaining an existing note) and an unnumbered, unapproved invoice draft at the service price, or the payer fee schedule when signed CPT units already exist. It records no treatment and takes no payment. The practitioner saves and signs the note; the billing desk reviews, refreshes from the signed note when needed, and approves the exact invoice under `BALM-001`, `BALM-003`, `CHART-004` and `CHART-006`. Repeated or concurrent status changes cannot start duplicate work. Visit details show the complete appointment change history with actor and clinic-local time, including changes made to the rest of a series. **Acceptance evidence:** Book, check in and mark attended, then mark another visit no-show and another canceled; each visit's history lists every change and its actor.

- **SCHED-009 — Calendar subscription.** Each practitioner subscribes their own schedule in Google Calendar, Outlook or Apple Calendar through a private, revocable iCalendar feed carrying times and initials only, never a diagnosis or full name. **Acceptance evidence:** Subscribe to the feed, book and cancel a visit, see the feed follow; revoke the feed and see the old address refused.

- **SCHED-010 — Practitioner capacity and double-booking.** Each practitioner has a concurrent-patient capacity, and a service can set its own lower one (for example 2 for a PT follow-up where an aide supervises the patient finishing exercises, 1 for an evaluation); both default to 1, which keeps `BALM-002`'s overlap refusal as published. Staff (owner, reception and the practitioner in their own calendar) book up to the lower of the two at overlapping times; the booking that would go past it is refused in the same D1 batch that inserts it, so two people racing for the last place get exactly one booking. Moves, recurring series (`SCHED-003`) and waitlist offers (`SCHED-005`) count capacity the same way, and a room or equipment a visit needs is still refused when taken. Online and portal booking (`SCHED-001`, `SCHED-006`) offer only times when the practitioner has no live visit, unless the practice turns on online double-booking for a service, in which case they offer times with capacity left. When a booking or move would overlap two Medicare Part B patients for one practitioner, Balm warns before saving that one-on-one timed codes cannot be billed for both patients over the same minutes (overlapping Medicare treatment bills as group therapy, 97150), and the person who books anyway is recorded on both visits. Migrated appointments (`MOVE-002` to `MOVE-005`) land as the source booked them, overlaps included, and any overlap beyond the configured capacity is named in the reconciliation (`MOVE-006`) rather than refused. **Exact rule:** at every instant inside a visit, the practitioner's booked or attended visits there, plus the new one, may not exceed the lowest of the practitioner's capacity and the capacity of every one of those visits' services (each 1 to 10, set by the owner); a visit is checked at its own start and at each later start of an overlapping visit, in code before saving and again in the D1 batch that writes it. A patient is a Medicare patient for a visit when their primary policy's payer is marked Medicare and its coverage includes the visit's clinic-local date. The Medicare warning applies to staff bookings, recurring series and moves (single and series); booking anyway records the acknowledging person and time on both visits and in both patients' access audits. Doors with no staff decision (online and portal booking, patient moves and waitlist acceptance) never place a Medicare patient over another Medicare patient's minutes. A migration names each visit that would exceed the mapped practitioner's and service's capacity, with both source rows. **Acceptance evidence:** Set one PT's capacity to 2; as reception book two patients at 9:00 and see both succeed and show side by side; book a third at 9:00 and be refused; race two staff bookings for the last place and see exactly one succeed; open `/book` and the portal for that PT and see 9:00 not offered; book two Medicare patients at 10:00 for the PT and see the warning before saving and the override recorded on both visits; import a rehabilitation EHR scheduler fixture with an overlapping pair for a practitioner whose capacity is 1 and see both visits land and the pair named in the reconciliation.

### Intake and patient portal

- **INTAKE-002 — Intake packets and outcome questionnaires.** The practice builds versioned forms and packets per service and discipline (PT, OT, SLP, pediatric), including standard outcome questionnaires (LEFS, Oswestry, Neck Disability Index, QuickDASH, PSFS) scored automatically into the chart. A new version never changes past answers. A service packet takes priority over a discipline packet, then the clinic default; pediatric services have an explicit intake discipline. The practice supplies its authorized standard questionnaire item wording in standard order. Balm stores the instrument, item order, scoring version and computed score with the immutable response: LEFS sum (0–80), Oswestry/NDI percentage of the answered maximum, QuickDASH mean adjusted to 0–100 (at least 10 of 11 items), and PSFS mean (0–10). Scores use the existing treating-clinician/owner intake access and audit. **Acceptance evidence:** Publish a PT packet with the LEFS, answer it as a patient, see the score in the chart, publish a new version and see earlier answers still bound to the old one.

- **INTAKE-003 — Signed consents and policies.** Patients sign consent to treat, the HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices acknowledgment, the financial and cancellation policy, telehealth consent and SMS consent, each with a drawn or typed signature, the time, the signer (patient, parent or guardian) and the exact document version, stored as a fixed PDF in the chart. **Acceptance evidence:** Sign each consent as a parent for a minor, download the fixed PDF, change the policy and see the patient asked to re-sign the new version only.

- **PORTAL-001 — Patient portal.** Patients sign in by email link or SMS code to see upcoming and past visits, forms to complete, their home program, messages, statements, receipts and superbills, and to update contact, insurance and card details. - **PORTAL-002 — Home exercise programs.** Clinicians build a home program from Balm's shipped exercise library (`PORTAL-007`) or their own exercises, each with images or video, sets, reps, hold and frequency, assign it from the note, and patients follow it in the portal or as a printable PDF and log completion and pain; adherence shows in the chart. **Acceptance evidence:** On a fresh install with no practice-made exercises, assign a 5-exercise program from the shipped library in a daily note, open it as the patient, log three sessions, and see adherence in the next note.

### Clinical documentation

- **CHART-002 — PT, OT and SLP templates.** Discipline templates cover the initial evaluation, daily note, progress report, re-evaluation and discharge summary for physical, occupational and speech therapy, with body-region and diagnosis variants, pick lists and practice-customized versions that never change signed notes. Each note type has required elements, and signing is refused while one is empty, naming it. The evaluation requires history (presenting condition, onset and mechanism, prior level of function, medical history, medications), systems review, tests and measures for the discipline (PT: range of motion, strength, special tests, gait and balance; OT: ADL and IADL performance, upper-extremity and fine motor function, cognition screen; SLP: standardized speech, language, swallowing or voice assessment), assessment with diagnoses and prognosis, measurable short- and long-term goals, a plan with frequency, duration and interventions, and the evaluation complexity code (97161–97163, 97165–97167, or the SLP evaluation code). The daily note requires subjective report, each intervention with its CPT code, minutes and parameters, the patient's response, assessment and plan for the next visit, and total timed and treatment minutes. The progress report and re-evaluation require objective measures compared with the evaluation (`CHART-005`), each goal's status (met, partly met, not met), the justification for continued skilled care and the updated plan. The discharge summary requires final measures, each goal's status, the reason for discharge and the home program. Every new note in an episode carries forward from the last signed one the diagnoses, goals and their status, precautions (`CHART-010`), the flowsheet's last values (`CHART-005`) and the plan, and never the subjective or assessment text. **Acceptance evidence:** Run the same journey for PT, OT and SLP: sign an evaluation, three daily notes, a progress report and a discharge summary in that discipline's templates. Each daily note opens with the diagnoses, goals, precautions and flowsheet carried forward and the subjective blank; signing a daily note with an intervention missing its minutes, and an evaluation without goals, is refused with the element named; the progress report and discharge show each goal's status; changing a template leaves every signed note byte-identical.

- **CHART-003 — Plan of care and physician certification.** The evaluation produces a plan of care with ICD-10 diagnoses, short- and long-term goals, frequency, duration and interventions, signed by the treating therapist. Balm sends it to the referring physician or non-physician practitioner by a private e-signature link, by fax through `REF-002`, or both, and records each send with its channel, recipient, date and, for a fax, the delivery confirmation. **Locked once sent:** from its first send, by fax or link, a plan's content and digest never change, which the database enforces. A change after sending makes a new version of the plan, linked to the version it replaces and marked as superseding it, which must be signed by the therapist and sent again; the earlier version keeps its sends and signature state, and its open link stops working. **Certification returns two ways:** the physician signs through the link, or a person files a returned signed copy, either an incoming fax (`REF-002`) or an uploaded signed scan, as the certification of a named plan version. Filing records the signing physician (name, credentials and NPI when shown), the signature date written on the copy, the certification date, and who filed it and when; it keeps the signed image in the chart as a signed plan of care document against that plan version and its episode (`CHART-009`); it is audited; and a copy filed to a version already signed is refused. **Signature state:** each plan version is **not sent**, **sent, pending**, **signed** or **late**. Late means not yet signed (not sent or pending) once a practice-set number of days has passed since the episode's first treatment visit, defaulting to Medicare's 30 days; a plan signed after that keeps its signature date and shows how late it was. **Work list:** a *Plans of care to sign* list shows every plan not sent, pending or late, with its physician, last send and age, and re-sends by link or fax in one action, which `AGENT-001` can prepare for a person to approve; the daily close (`REPORT-004`) lists pending and late plans, and `CHART-007` warns before a note is signed for a visit under a late or uncertified plan. Balm records the certification period (from the start of care for the plan's duration, at most 90 days), tracks the 90-day recertification due date, flags visits past it, and applies any state direct-access limit. Its goals, with their status, carry into each progress report and the discharge summary (`CHART-002`). Before returned-plan certification ships, file each signed fax/scan as an immutable CHART-009 original against its named plan version and episode, find it in chart history, and retain that linkage through export/restore. **Acceptance evidence:** Sign an evaluation and its plan of care, send it by fax, and see it locked and marked pending with the fax confirmation. Edit it and see a new version that must be sent again, the sent version unchanged. Receive the physician's signed fax, file it to that plan version, and see the plan signed with the signer, the signature date, the certification date and the signed image attached in the chart; file a second copy to it and be refused. Send another plan by link and sign it as the physician. Leave a third plan unsigned 30 days past its first treatment and see it marked late on the work list and the daily close; set the practice's threshold to 21 days and see a plan 22 days past its first treatment marked late. Let a certified plan pass its recertification date and see the next visit flagged. Every added plan version, fax-send and returned-certification door must update the owner’s audited REPORT-004 daily close before it ships; pending, late and expired plan work must appear and clear from its recorded outcome. Find each newly added document in chart history, filter it by its episode when episodes exist, and reopen its original bytes as an entitled clinician.

- **CHART-004 — Draft, sign, lock and amend.** Notes are drafts until the clinician signs; signing locks the note with the signer, time and credentials; changes after signing are addenda or amendments that keep the signed original visible; unsigned notes past the practice's deadline appear on the daily close. **Acceptance evidence:** Sign a note, try to edit it and be offered an addendum, add one, and read both the original and the addendum; an unsigned note past 24 hours appears on the daily close.

- **CHART-005 — Objective measures and flowsheets.** Clinicians record range of motion, manual muscle tests, girth, balance and gait tests, standardized scores and exercise flowsheets in structured fields, compare them with the evaluation and graph them over the episode. Before measures ship, care-team grants and break glass (`CHART-013`) read them as they read notes, with the same audit. A measure is recorded in a treatment note by that note's author while it is a draft and locks with the note's signature; whoever reads the note reads its measures under the note's read audit, and reception and agents read none. Each kind has fixed units and bounds (range of motion in degrees; manual muscle test grades 0–5 with + or −, stored in quarter steps; girth in cm or in; balance and gait tests in seconds, points, m/s, meters or feet; standardized scores in points or percent; flowsheet load in lb, kg or bodyweight with sets and reps), and values keep at most two decimals. A series is one measure name (ignoring case), side and unit in visit order; its evaluation value is the earliest entry recorded in an evaluation note, or its first entry when no evaluation recorded it, and the change is the later value minus that one. Progress and discharge notes show each series' evaluation value, the value at that visit and the change. A series is kept per episode of care (`REF-003`): its evaluation value, change and graph use only entries from visits in the same episode, and entries not assigned to an episode form their own series. **Acceptance evidence:** Record knee flexion and LEFS at evaluation and three later visits; the progress report shows the change and the graph matches the entries.

- **CHART-006 — Billing captured from the note.** The note records each CPT code with timed and untimed minutes; Balm calculates billable units by the timed-unit method of the payer the charge bills to (`INS-001`): CMS total-time (the Medicare 8-minute rule), AMA/CPT per-service substantial portion, or the practice's fixed per-unit threshold for that payer. Under CMS total-time, units come from the pooled timed minutes and are distributed by CMS's rule: each code first takes its whole 15-minute units, then the remaining units go to the codes with the most remaining minutes, and on a tie the clinician chooses which code takes the unit, with the choice recorded. Where an equal-remainder group straddles the last available units, the clinician selects exactly as many codes in that group as there are units left; new signatures refuse an unresolved choice. The selection is part of the immutable revision and its signed digest, and only breaks equal remainders; AMA and fixed methods ignore it. Under AMA/CPT each timed code is counted on its own, with a unit for each full 15 minutes and one more when the remainder is 8 minutes or more. Under a fixed threshold each code bills a unit only for each full threshold of minutes. The charge records the method and the payer setting it used. When the balance moves to a secondary or tertiary payer (`INS-013`) whose method differs, Balm re-applies that payer's method to the note's minutes for its claim, keeps the primary's line adjudication per code, and shows both unit counts on the charge. Balm applies GP, GO or GN, 59 or X{EPSU}, KX, CQ or CO modifiers where they apply, links ICD-10 pointers, and sends the signed charge to billing. A clinician can select up to four recorded visit ICD-10 codes per CPT in the note; the immutable revision stores code identities, not list positions. Saving and signing refuse selections absent from that visit. Signed charges for the same CPT combine their distinct selected codes; a claim refuses more than four or a code since removed, and reordering visit diagnoses recomputes pointers without retargeting the signed selections. Issued invoice snapshots retain the selected codes and diagnosis list for per-visit and period superbills. An omitted selection retains the existing default (first visit diagnosis for a claim, all visit diagnoses for a superbill). **Acceptance evidence:** Document 38 minutes across 97110 and 97140 and one untimed 97161 for a Medicare patient; the charge shows 3 timed units distributed by the rule, the right modifiers and diagnosis pointers. Sign the same note with 8 minutes of 97110 and 8 minutes of 97140 for a CMS-method payer and an AMA-method payer: the first charge bills 1 unit, to the code the clinician picks on the tie, and the second bills 2 units, one per code. With 10 minutes of 97110 and 8 of 97140, the CMS charge bills its 1 unit to 97110. Under a fixed 15-minute threshold the 8-and-8 note bills no timed unit and the scrubber names both codes. A charge whose primary uses the CMS method and whose secondary uses the AMA method sends the secondary claim with 2 units and the primary with 1.

- **CHART-007 — Documentation compliance alerts.** Before signing, Balm warns when a progress report is due (Medicare: by the 10th treatment day), the plan of care is late, uncertified or expired (`CHART-003`), the KX threshold is near, an assistant's visit needs supervision, or a required element for the payer is missing. **Acceptance evidence:** Reach the 10th visit without a progress report, an expired certification and a KX-threshold patient; each warning appears before signing and on the daily close. Configure a payer that requires total treatment minutes on every daily note, leave them out, and see signing blocked with the element and the payer named.

- **CHART-008 — Supervision and co-signature.** Students, clinical fellows and assistants document under a named supervisor; their notes wait in the supervisor's co-sign queue, are billed under the supervisor where payer rules require, and show both signatures. Co-signed notes keep `CHART-013` care-team and break-glass read access and audit. **ct-approved baseline (22 October 2026):** an author signature freezes the exact revision and measures until the named active licensed supervisor returns it with an immutable reason or co-signs; a return requires a new author revision and signature. Submission alone gives the supervisor only that note, not the wider chart. The payer’s owner-recorded rendering rule defaults to the supervisor, and conflicting supervisors on one visit refuse billing. An assistant may finalize only a daily treatment note under an explicit owner-recorded rule naming that assistant, the applicable state, current payer (or cash), covered care dates and both permission sources; there is no permission by default, and revocation closes future signing. Their signature stays immutable and claiming still waits for a separate immutable, signed statement of actual supervision from the named therapist; payer billability/minimum/rendering rules still apply. Students and fellows always require co-signature. The named supervisor is an active licensed therapist of the author's discipline; at co-signature the supervisor records the supervision given (in the room, on site or general), and each payer's owner-recorded rule decides whether that visit is billable and under whom. Medicare student care requires the therapist in the room; Medicare students and assistants bill under the supervisor. Unrecorded discipline, supervision or payer rules refuse claiming. `ROLE-002` and `AGENT-003` inherit these authority and signature constraints. **Acceptance evidence:** Document as a student, co-sign as the supervisor, and see the note locked with both signatures and the claim under the supervisor.

- **CHART-009 — Attachments and searchable history.** Referrals, imaging reports, physician orders, signed plans of care returned by fax or scan (`CHART-003`) and photos attach to the chart; clinicians search the patient's history by text, date, document type and episode. Uploaded JPEG, PNG, WebP and PDF originals (up to 1 MB each), their title, type, document date and entered searchable description are immutable. The owner reads and uploads for every patient; a treating practitioner (one with a visit for that patient) uploads as themselves and reads/searches all of that patient’s uploaded chart documents, whoever filed them. ### Reporting and accounting

- **REPORT-001 — Operational reports.** Owners see visits, arrivals, cancellations and no-shows, plan-of-care completion (visits per episode, drop-off), new evaluations, practitioner utilization and productivity, referral conversion and visit counts for payroll, filtered by date, location and practitioner, and export every report as CSV. Only the owner sees these reports; every figure is an aggregate per practitioner with an all-practitioner total, never naming patients. Current location attribution follows the visit’s room for visits/evaluations, owner-assigned weekly hours for availability, the source note’s visit for a plan (latest plan for an episode), and the first episode visit for referral conversion. Unknown locations are explicitly Unassigned, including older anonymous facts; plan completion still counts the whole course, and these opaque location dimensions survive approved destruction. A date range is whole clinic-local dates in the clinic's time zone, inclusive at both ends, and a visit counts on the local date it starts. Visits count every visit by its current status (still booked, arrived, canceled, no-show); the no-show rate is no-shows out of arrivals plus no-shows. An episode of care (`REF-003`) with a plan of care counts once, in the range its first plan starts in: its planned visits (frequency times weeks) come from its latest-starting plan (ties: the higher plan id), whose therapist the row belongs to, and it counts every attended visit filed to the episode. A plan not assigned to an episode is one episode by itself: it counts attended visits with its own therapist, outside any episode, from its start date through the last day of its planned weeks. Either is completed once attended visits reach its planned visits, and dropped off when fewer were attended and no visit of that episode (or, for an unassigned plan, with that therapist) is booked after now. New evaluations are signed initial-evaluation notes, dated by their visit (or by signing when no visit is linked). Available time is each practitioner's current weekly hours on every date in the range except clinic holidays, in wall-clock minutes; utilization is booked, attended and no-show minutes out of available minutes, productivity is attended minutes out of available minutes, and rates are percentages rounded to one decimal. Visit counts for payroll are attended visits and their booked minutes by practitioner and service. This clause is done when every capability that exists today conforms; `ROLE-002` carries the current room-location filter before its location manager ships; `SCHED-007` inherits it for future location/availability doors; referral conversion is done under `REF-001` and episode counting under `REF-003`. **Acceptance evidence:** Run a month of synthetic visits and see each figure match a hand count; export each report and reopen it.

- **REPORT-002 — Financial reports.** Charges, payments, adjustments, A/R aging by payer and patient, collections per visit, days in A/R and deposit reconciliation, exported as CSV. - **HIPAA-002 — Retention and destruction.** Health records are kept for the retention period the practice's state requires (with minors kept until majority plus the state's period). The later of the adult deadline and majority plus the applicable minor period applies; later clinical entries extend retention, and leap-day anniversaries use March 1. Unresolved state rules fail closed: an ambiguous sourced rule needs the owner’s documented longer rule, and an unresearched rule cannot authorize destruction. Every row of the table is reviewed against its primary statute or regulation (never a secondary summary alone) and names its section and retrieval date; a row is verified only when that source gives complete adult and minor periods for the whole PT/OT/SLP practice, and otherwise it is ambiguous, including when the source is silent on duration. A period stated without a starting point (Indiana’s seven years) is counted from the last treatment. A preview expires after 15 minutes and its digest binds the exact inventory, policy and record version. Approved destruction removes clinical content, patient identifiers and private tokens, scrubs patient free text from audit details only, and keeps anonymous dated business facts, immutable audit sequence/action/time/actor/opaque references and a destruction receipt. ERASE-001’s latest-journal/destination-known restore fence applies; a fresh destination without later erasure evidence cannot know later destruction. There is no universal seven-year HIPAA retention period. **Acceptance evidence:** Choose the practice's state and see its adult and minor periods applied from the shipped table with the source named; set a 7-year retention, try to erase a treated patient and be refused, advance past retention, approve destruction and see the destruction record.

## Push further with agents

Every agent here works through `BALM-001`: an owner-bound key with a declared scope, a prepare-then-approve fence that records the digest of what a person approved, a visible pause, and an audit row for every read. Agents never sign notes, change a plan of care, submit an appeal, refund, write off or message a new recipient without the approval named in their clause.

- **AGENT-001 — Referral-to-collection case agent.** For each referral the agent follows the case from referral documents and consent through eligibility, authorization, plan-of-care certification, scheduled and attended visits, signed notes, claims, remittances and patient balance. It keeps a dated next-action list with cited evidence (the fax page, the 277CA line, the unsigned note), prepares the next permitted step (re-send a pending or late plan of care by fax or link from `CHART-003`'s work list, chase the payer, ask the patient for a card), sends only through approved channels and templates, and closes the case only when the claim and patient balance are reconciled. Clinicians confirm every clinical assertion; billing staff approve coding, appeals and financial exceptions. Before the case agent prepares, attaches or transmits a prior authorization packet, or adds any prior authorization transport through Availity, a payer prior-authorization interface, fax or email, it inherits INS-004's clinical authority, exact-digest custody and recorded-submission rules; its owner-approved re-authorization emails appear in the referral's INS-004 request history and take their decision through INS-004's decision door. Electronic transmission needs the practice's clearinghouse enrollment and a recorded business associate agreement; that is engineering and agreement work in this clause, not a reason to narrow it. **Acceptance evidence:** Run a referral whose certification stalls and whose claim is denied; the agent cites each blocker, prepares the follow-ups for approval, waits on unapproved steps, and shows the case closed only after the paid ERA and zero patient balance.

- **AGENT-002 — Care continuity agent.** From cancellations, the waitlist, clinician capacity, authorization limits and plan-of-care dates, the agent proposes replacement visit sequences that keep the plan's frequency inside the authorization, offers them to patients after a clinician approves any change in cadence, and verifies the booking and coverage together. **Acceptance evidence:** Cancel two visits in a patient's plan; the agent proposes a sequence inside the authorization, the clinician approves, the patient accepts by text, and the bookings and remaining authorization agree.

- **AGENT-003 — Documentation defensibility check.** Before a note is signed, the agent reads the note, plan of care, billed minutes and the payer's documentation rules and lists what an auditor would question (skilled need not shown, units not supported, missing progress report), each with the rule cited; the clinician decides every change. **Acceptance evidence:** Draft a note billing 4 units with 40 minutes of mostly unskilled exercise; the agent names the unsupported unit and the missing skilled rationale with sources, and the clinician's edit clears them. The result identifies the exact note revision and plan/payer-rule snapshot, with rule citations and record excerpts for each question. An edit retains the previous result as stale; rechecking uses the edited revision. The check never edits, signs, certifies or claims payer validation. - **AGENT-004 — Denial resolver.** For each rejection and denial the agent reads the 277CA or 835 codes, the claim, note and authorization, explains the cause in plain language, and prepares the corrected claim or appeal letter with the evidence attached; a biller approves before anything is sent, and the agent watches for the payment. **Acceptance evidence:** Feed a sandbox denial for a missing authorization; the agent prepares an appeal citing the authorization and note, the biller approves, and the agent marks it resolved only when payment posts.

## Honest boundaries

- **Not a clearinghouse or payer network.** Balm submits through Claim.MD and Availity under the practice's own enrollment; payer enrollment and credentialing (CAQH, PECOS) remain the practice's or its credentialing service's work, with Balm tracking the status per provider and payer.
- **Not a card processor or lender.** Card data stays with Stripe; funds settle to the practice's Stripe account; patient financing goes through a partner such as Cherry or CareCredit.
- **Not HIPAA compliance by hosting.** No host makes a practice compliant on its own. Balm names deployments whose hosting BAA a small practice can obtain and refuses real patient data until that BAA is recorded (`HIPAA-005`), records the practice's other BAAs, risk analysis and officers, and keeps BAA-dependent integrations off until they exist (`HIPAA-001`); the practice signs its own BAAs and remains the covered entity.
- **Not clinical judgment.** Clinicians decide diagnosis, medical necessity, plan of care and every statement in a signed note; agents and the scribe draft and check.
- **Not a phone system, payroll or HR system.** Calls run on the practice's phone provider; payroll and 1099/W-2 filing run in Gusto or ADP ([Envelope](https://github.com/just-every/run-envelope) is the RunEditRun payroll sibling); staff HR records live in Willow or BambooHR.
- **Not DME supplier billing, e-prescribing or lab and imaging orders.** Products sell for cash under `PAY-008`; insurance billing for durable medical equipment goes through a DME billing partner.
- **US first.** USD, US payers, US time zones and US privacy law first; other countries' claiming systems (Medicare Australia, NHS) are not part of this target.
